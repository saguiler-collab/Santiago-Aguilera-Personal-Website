Beyond the kit

Glockenspiel, piano, the steel pan, a mixing desk, and now my voice.

A drummer is a percussionist, and percussion has a lot of relatives. At ISPS my music teachers kept handing me an instrument I had never touched and asking what I would do with it. Each one changed how I play the kit.

01

Glockenspiel

The glockenspiel is a percussion instrument that carries a melody, which my journal describes as “basically drums combined with piano”. In Grade 9 I chose it for the school song because nobody else in the class was playing it, and it let me keep a drummer's hands while I learned to carry a tune.

  • The school songLearned on glockenspiel, then played at International Day 2023 in the gym with the 10th graders, glockenspiel at the front and the drums at the back.
  • Legally BlondeFirst assigned the brass riffs of “Chip on My Shoulder” on it.
02

Piano

In Music II I moved the same school song from the glockenspiel to the piano: two hands doing different jobs, keys that all look alike, and about two weeks to switch. I worked out how to play it without moving my hands, by fixing my fingers in set positions. The school-song summative came back an 8 out of 8.

  • BeforePiano lessons at six or seven, at beginner level.
  • Grade 10The school song, assessed first as a formative, then as the summative: an 8.
  • Legally BlondePiano with a backing track on “Chip on My Shoulder”.
03

Steel pan

The steel pan is Trinidad's instrument, and the one I had never played. In January 2024 the school hosted visiting students from Alaska, and our class learned “Engine Room”, a Trinidad and Tobago piece, to play with them. I took the tenor pan, which carries the lead melody.

  • The first classI got past my first goal, the opening line of the sheet, in a single lesson.
  • The pressureSeveral classmates left for a volleyball trip, which left me holding the lead melody on an instrument I had been playing for two weeks.
  • The visitorsThey had no drummer, so I played drums on some of their songs, and we all played “Waka Waka” together.
  • The habit to unlearnAs a drummer I hit the pan too hard. Playing it taught me to play with less force.
04

Behind the mixing desk

Not every music class was about playing. In Grade 9 the Sound Off! unit asked how communication can reach an audience in different ways, and the answer was audio engineering. For a few weeks the person next to the drum kit was the person at the board.

  • The theoryInput, signal processing and output; the signal chain; dynamic, condenser and ribbon microphones; and the order you switch a mixing desk on and off.
  • The workshopsFour, swapping between playing in the band and engineering the school theatre's analog board. The first one was a mess: we never got a piece played.
  • The assessmentsA written exam in December, then a group practical that I called the hardest summative of my music career at ISPS.
  • What I took from itI had planned the whole practical in my head and told my group too late. Share the plan before the clock starts, and let people do their roles.
  • The field tripQueen's Hall in Port of Spain, to see a fully digital sound setup next to our own.
05

Voice

I never sang at ISPS. At Perkiomen I have sung with the rock band, so the kit is no longer the only thing I do on stage. It is a different muscle. In Grade 9, while we prepared Legally Blonde, our class covered what vocalists need from a band: speak-singing, clear enunciation, steady intonation and listening to the players around them. For years I was the one keeping time for the singers.

  • Rock bandDrums, and vocals. Singing began at Perkiomen.
  • From Grade 9What a singer needs from the band, learned in the Legally Blonde unit.
