ISPS School Musicals

Beauty and the Beast, Legally Blonde and Mamma Mia!: three pits, three years.

Every year the ISPS music class plays the pit for the school musical. I played in three of them, and each asked something different of a drummer. The journals I kept for each unit are where this page comes from.

Shows
Beauty and the Beast, Legally Blonde, Mamma Mia!
Years
2023, 2024, 2025
Instruments
Drums, piano
01

Beauty and the Beast · Grade 8 · May 2023

The earliest show in my journals. We had about a month to learn the drum parts, which I practised alongside the class song “As It Was”. It taught me to split my time between a group piece and a show with an audience.

  • The piecesDrum parts including “Gaston” and “Maurice's Entrance”.
  • The lessonTwo communities at once: the music class, and the production's music crew.
02

Legally Blonde · Grade 9 · June 2024

The official song list gave me five drum pieces and one piano part with a backing track. The 10th graders asked me to drum their number, “Whipped into Shape”, which I took as an honour. My fellow drummer and I split the other drum parts so we both got to play, and shared one kit through the rehearsals.

  • Drums“What You Want” (reggae), “Positive”, “Harvard Party Music”, “Whipped into Shape”, “Legally Blonde Remix”.
  • Piano with track“Chip on My Shoulder”.
  • ShowsJune 6 and 7, after weeks of rehearsals and a dress rehearsal the day before.
  • What heldTransitions between pieces got smoother from Thursday to Friday, and the pit ran without technical problems.
  • What did not“What You Want” under pressure in the verses: I kept the beat but not every note.
03

Mamma Mia! · Grade 10 · June 2025

This one was a different level. The score was played strictly from the sheet, with professional musicians in the band, so there was no room to invent my own versions of the songs as in earlier shows. I prepared eleven pieces over six weeks, and I was asked to play two of them on stage.

  • Prepared“Money, Money, Money” (learned in a single double period), “Dancing Queen”, “Waterloo”, “The Winner Takes It All”, “Thank You for the Music”, “Mamma Mia”, a scene change, and more. Eleven in all.
  • On stage“Lay All Your Love on Me” and “Voulez-Vous”, June 11 and 12.
  • What I learnedThat show-day pressure is its own skill. After the first night I wrote down exactly what slipped (tempo, balance, the rests) so I could correct it for the second.
04

What the three have in common

In a band the drummer sets the groove. In a musical the groove belongs to the score, the singers and the people on stage, and the drummer's job is to keep time for all of them. Over three years that moved from playing by ear to playing from a sheet, from many parts to the two I could be trusted with, and from working alone to working with a pit.
